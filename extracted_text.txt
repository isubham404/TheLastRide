Project vision
The Last Beacon is a story-driven 2D science-fiction platformer in which players guide molecular biologist and
astronaut Ryland Grace from a research campus at SUIIT into deep space. There, he encounters Rocky, an intelligent
alien engineer. Together, they investigate a cosmic biological threat and attempt to protect two civilisations.
Design direction: cinematic pixel art with atmospheric lighting, expressive character animation, environmental storytelling and puzzles
rooted in science and cooperation.

1. Executive Summary
The Last Beacon is planned as a single-player, 2D side-scrolling adventure with platforming, exploration, survival
systems and logic-based puzzles. Its central mechanic is the ability to switch between two characters whose abilities
complement one another. Ryland uses scientific analysis and human technology; Rocky uses alien engineering,
climbing and physical strength.
The experience begins at SUIIT, establishing a distinctive Earth-based opening before expanding into a damaged
interstellar spacecraft, deep space, an alien ecosystem and the Eridian civilisation. The narrative progresses from
scientific curiosity to first contact, cooperation, sacrifice and a new sense of home.
Project goals
 Distinctive setting: use a recognisable SUIIT-inspired campus as the opening location.
 Meaningful mechanics: make science and teamwork the primary tools for progress.
 Emotional storytelling: build the relationship between the two protagonists through playable moments.
 Manageable production: develop a polished vertical slice before expanding to the complete game.

4. Main Characters
Ryland Grace — Human scientist and astronaut. Ryland is the primary scientific problem-solver. His abilities
include scanning, biological analysis, operating human technology, using tools and limited jetpack movement.
Rocky — Alien engineer. Rocky provides a different perspective on technology and the environment. His abilities
include climbing designated surfaces, moving heavy objects, repairing alien machinery, interpreting sound patterns
and accessing narrow passages.
Character switching
The player switches between Ryland and Rocky using a dedicated input (proposed default: TAB). The active character
is shown in the HUD. Puzzles should communicate clearly which character or ability is needed, while allowing optional
experimentation.
5. Core Mechanics

Run, jump, climb where permitted, interact with objects and traverse platforming routes.
Scanner and journal
Ryland scans organisms, machinery and anomalies. Findings are stored as clues and research entries.
Character cooperation
Switch characters to reach different routes, operate paired mechanisms and solve multi-stage puzzles.
Puzzle interaction
Molecular sequencing, signal matching, circuit repair, gravity routing and environmental logic.
Resource management
Selected sequences use oxygen, energy, fuel and repair components. Resources should support tension,
not create constant frustration.
Dialogue and translation
Rocky's musical communication becomes progressively understandable through a translation mechanic.
Progression
Research upgrades unlock new scanning, traversal, translation and engineering capabilities.
6. Story Structure and Chapter Plan
Chapter 01 — The Signal from Earth
Location: SUIIT campus and research laboratory
Story: Ryland investigates an unusual meteorite sample and detects a mysterious biological signature. A solar
anomaly leads to an emergency recruitment message.
Level sequence: Campus exploration; laboratory interaction; scanner tutorial; molecular puzzle; antenna repair and
signal decoding.
Gameplay purpose: The player learns movement, interaction, scanning and basic puzzle logic.
Chapter 02 — The Hail Mary
Location: Earth orbit and spacecraft
Story: Ryland boards the interstellar spacecraft. A crisis damages systems and leaves him disoriented. He must
restore essential systems and navigate an asteroid field.
Level sequence: Launch sequence; oxygen emergency; power restoration; navigation puzzle; asteroid-dodging
sequence; unknown contact.
Gameplay purpose: Introduces spacecraft survival, resource management and navigation challenges.

Location: Alien ecosystem and research sites
Story: The pair investigate a biological ecosystem to identify a natural predator or countermeasure for the cosmic

Chapter 05 — The Cost of Friendship
Location: Deep space and damaged spacecraft
Story: A contamination and mechanical emergency threaten the research and Rocky's ship. Ryland must choose to
help his friend rather than prioritise a safe return.
Level sequence: Containment; engine repair; resource collection; rescue sequence; route decision and emotional
climax.
Gameplay purpose: Combines previously learned mechanics under pressure and delivers the major emotional
turning point.
Chapter 06 — The Last Beacon
Location: Erid and the Eridian civilisation
Story: Rocky returns home with the discovery. Grace helps complete the final experiment and transmit the solution to
Earth, then finds a new role among the Eridians.
Level sequence: Explore Erid; repair infrastructure; final biological experiment; activate beacon; transmit discovery;
closing reunion.
Gameplay purpose: Resolves the central mission and completes the friendship arc.

7. Opening Storyboard
The opening sequence should establish the scale of the threat and then bring the player into a familiar, grounded
environment. The first 90 seconds are designed as a cinematic hand-off into player control.
00:00–00:15 | The Warning
A dark screen fills with stars. Earth appears. A restrained voice-over hints that something is changing in the universe.
00:15–00:30 | SUIIT
The camera moves from Earth to the SUIIT-inspired campus at night. A single research laboratory remains lit.
00:30–00:45 | The Discovery
Ryland examines a meteorite sample. A biological signal appears on the monitor and the laboratory warns of an
unidentified organism.
00:45–01:00 | The Mission
An emergency transmission requests Ryland's presence. A star map highlights the distant mission destination.
01:00–01:15 | The Journey Begins
A launch montage follows the spacecraft leaving Earth. Music rises, then drops into silence.
01:15–01:30 | Wake Up
Ryland wakes inside a dark spacecraft as an alarm sounds. The camera settles into the playable side-scrolling view
and control is handed to the player.
8. Key Puzzle Concepts
Molecular reconstruction
Scan a sample, identify its components and arrange them in a correct sequence. Used in Chapters 1, 4 and 6.
Rocky's language
Listen to recurring musical patterns, compare their rhythm and frequency, and match them to meanings. Vocabulary
builds over time.
Circuit repair
Connect power sources and components to restore systems. Later versions introduce multiple power routes and
hazards.
Gravity challenge
Activate or redirect gravity systems to change platform positions and traversal routes.
Dual engineering
Use Ryland at a human console and Rocky at an alien mechanism to synchronise two systems.
Final biological experiment
Combine clues and discoveries from earlier chapters to stabilise the final countermeasure.

PART II — PRODUCTION PLAN
10. Visual and Audio Direction
Visual language: pixel-art characters and tile-based environments supported by cinematic lighting, parallax layers,
atmospheric particles and restrained glow effects. Each world should have a distinct palette: warm natural tones for
Earth, deep navy and electric blue for space, orange and red for hostile alien terrain, and luminous greens and warm
highlights for Erid.
Animation priorities: idle, walk, run, jump, fall, interact, scan, repair and damage states for Ryland; idle, move, climb,
lift, repair, signal and reaction states for Rocky. Prioritise readable silhouettes and expressive timing.
Audio: use environmental ambience in each location, mechanical sounds in the spacecraft, original musical motifs for
Rocky's communication, tension layers for emergencies and a warmer orchestral palette for the conclusion. Avoid
relying on licensed film music.
11. User Interface
 Top-left: active character portrait and health/status.
 Top-right: oxygen, energy or other chapter-specific resources.
 Bottom-left: current objective and optional hint.
 Bottom-right: character-switch indicator and relevant ability prompt.
 Contextual interaction prompt near usable objects; avoid clutter during exploration.
 Research journal and map accessible through pause or dedicated controls.
12. Technology and Project Structure
Engine: Godot 4. Language: GDScript. Art: Aseprite or Pixelorama. Tilemap planning: Tiled or Godot
TileMapLayer. Audio editing: Audacity. Use version control (for example, Git) and maintain a shared naming
convention for scenes, scripts and assets.

PART III — DELIVERY AND SUCCESS CRITERIA
15. First Playable Demo (Vertical Slice)
The recommended first release is a 20–30 minute demo that proves the game's visual identity, core movement,
scientific puzzle design and character partnership. It should contain three connected environments and end at the first
meaningful encounter with Rocky.
Demo sequence
1. SUIIT campus
Explore a small campus area, learn movement and enter the research laboratory.
2. Laboratory discovery
Scan the sample, solve the first molecular puzzle and receive the solar anomaly transmission.
3. Hail Mary
Transition to the spacecraft, restore a critical system and complete a short survival challenge.
4. Unknown contact
Detect and approach the alien ship.
5. First encounter
Meet Rocky, hear his musical signal and complete a simple communication interaction.
Demo acceptance criteria
 Player can complete the demo from start to finish without blocking bugs.
 Movement, collisions, interaction prompts and camera behaviour are reliable.
 The first scientific puzzle is understandable and has clear feedback.
 The story hand-off from SUIIT to space is coherent.
 The first encounter with Rocky creates a memorable ending hook.
 The game runs at a stable frame rate on the target development PC.
16. Risks and Mitigation
Risk
Mitigation
Scope becomes too large
Build a vertical slice first; lock chapter scope before producing all art and levels.
Too many unique assets
Reuse modular tiles, props and lighting setups while giving each world a distinct palette.
Puzzles become confusing
Use environmental clues, progressive hints, clear feedback and playtesting.
Character switching feels awkward
Prototype switching early; make character roles clear and avoid unnecessary switching.
Performance issues
Profile early, limit expensive effects and test on target hardware throughout production.
Intellectual-property concerns
Treat film characters and story as fan-game material; obtain rights or replace them with original
characters before public/commercial release.

18. Closing Concept Abstract
The Last Beacon is a cinematic 2D science-fiction adventure about discovery, cooperation and friendship across
worlds. Beginning at SUIIT, the game follows Ryland Grace from a mysterious laboratory discovery into an interstellar
mission. His encounter with Rocky transforms the journey from a solitary survival mission into a partnership between
two very different beings. Through platforming, exploration, scientific puzzles and environmental storytelling, players
uncover a biological mystery and work toward saving two civilisations.
The project's defining feature is its dual-character design: Ryland's scientific reasoning and Rocky's alien engineering
must work together to overcome obstacles neither could solve alone. With pixel-art environments, atmospheric
lighting, an evolving musical communication system and an emotionally driven chapter structure, The Last Beacon
aims to deliver a memorable indie-game experience with a distinctive university-inspired beginning.
